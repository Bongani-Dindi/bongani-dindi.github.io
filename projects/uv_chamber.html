01

The project

This project starts with a laboratory need: expand the materials-testing service by developing an ultraviolet ageing chamber. The proposed system will expose flat specimens to controlled UV radiation, heat and condensation so that changes such as fading, cracking and surface deterioration can be studied under recorded conditions.

My work connects the intended service to the engineering requirements. That includes selecting the test method and lamps, defining the usable specimen area, coordinating the enclosure and controls, and planning the evidence needed to demonstrate repeatable performance. The current record covers the proposal, requirements work and early technical investigations. Construction, commissioning and independent qualification remain later deliverables.

02

Project goals

  1. Define a clear test basis. Confirm the materials, specimen sizes, exposure methods, operating limits and acceptance checks before fixing the design.
  2. Create controlled exposure conditions. Develop a UVA-340 lamp arrangement, specimen holders, temperature measurement and control, and a repeatable condensation system.
  3. Make operation safe and traceable. Plan door interlocks, independent temperature protection, water-related fault responses, automated cycles and time-stamped test records.
  4. Prepare a verification and handover package. Bring together drawings, component records, calibration requirements, operating instructions and performance evidence for review and independent assessment.
03

Technical learning

From a service need to measurable requirements

I am learning to translate a broad business goal into checks that an engineer and a laboratory operator can use. “Uniform exposure” needs a defined measurement grid, usable sample area and acceptance criterion. “Repeatable testing” needs controlled cycles, calibrated measurements and records of interruptions. Defining the evidence alongside each requirement makes later design reviews more useful.

UV, heat and moisture behave as one system

Lamp choice affects the spectrum and exposure distribution; lamp spacing and specimen distance affect the geometry; airflow and specimen temperature affect the thermal conditions; the water system must create condensation on the exposed sample surfaces. I am learning to work through these interfaces before committing to enclosure dimensions or a control scheme.

Temperature measurement and test conditions

The project has prompted me to study black-panel temperature, sample exposure and the purpose of the selected weathering method. I am learning to connect each measured quantity to the test condition it represents, and to define sensor placement and calibration needs early. The operating cycle and its set points will follow the chosen material-specific method.

Risk-led verification

The planning work has helped me identify the questions that can change the whole design: suitable lamps, adequate UV distribution, stable temperature control and reliable condensation. Early modelling and subsystem tests are planned around those questions. The later verification report will need measured performance, safety checks and representative cycle records.

04

Soft skills & working habits

  • Scope and prioritisation. I am practising how to define a useful first version, explain its boundaries and keep optional capabilities from delaying the core chamber. The proposal turns those decisions into a short scope, goals and deliverable list.
  • Communicating with different reviewers. Writing a short employer-facing proposal has helped me explain the same project at different levels: the service it enables, the capabilities an operator needs, and the evidence a technical reviewer will expect.
  • Planning with feedback. I am learning to use weekly reviews to revisit requirements, risks, supplier information and technical results. Linking each phase to an observable output makes it easier to raise uncertainty and adjust the plan before it affects downstream work.
05

Detailed updates

A record of documented work and learning through September 2026. Future verification and delivery steps are listed separately.

September 2026

Requirements and early risk questions

The early work focused on intended materials, sample capacity, standards, facilities and the subsystem boundaries. Lamp suitability, exposure distribution, temperature control and condensation were identified as questions to investigate early.

Learning: a requirement becomes more useful when its verification method is defined at the same time. This has also improved how I explain unresolved choices to a reviewer.

12 September 2026

A clearer proposal and capability description

The proposal was developed into a professional project description with scope, goals, a phased timeline and the intended chamber capabilities. It also identifies the design, controls, performance-testing and handover documents that the project will need.

Deliverable: a proposal and capability description prepared for review. Approval, construction and performance verification are separate milestones.

September 2026

Connecting the chamber to the lamp model

A companion irradiance calculator is being used to explore the lamp-array design questions. This creates a way to compare geometry choices before finalising the mechanical layout, then define what must be measured on the physical system.

Next evidence: a measured UV map and temperature/condensation tests to support the later design and qualification decisions.
