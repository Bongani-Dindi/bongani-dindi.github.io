01

The project

This study examines how mechanical constraints and changing thermal conditions can load a piping and flange assembly. The global model describes the overall load path and support reactions; the local model provides a more detailed view of the flange, fasteners and contact conditions.

My work includes reconstructing parametric geometry, defining load cases, documenting uncertain inputs and planning sensitivity studies. The engineering task is to establish a credible modelling basis and understand which assumptions can change the interpretation. Mesh checks, load-transfer verification and sensitivity results remain part of the evidence needed before final conclusions.

02

Project goals

  1. Build a defensible model basis. Document the geometry, materials, reference temperature, operating states, supports and boundaries used in the analysis.
  2. Track the governing load path. Represent thermal, pressure, weight and restraint effects, then extract forces and moments at a documented transfer station.
  3. Understand the local joint response. Investigate flange contact, bolt-preload assumptions, separation and local stress with an appropriate joint representation.
  4. Test the sensitivity of the interpretation. Check mesh behaviour, support cases, thermal assumptions and preload ranges before deciding which conclusions the available evidence supports.
03

Technical learning

Thermal loading and boundary conditions

I am learning to connect temperature change and the stress-free reference state to thermal deformation, then examine how restraints turn that deformation into reactions. Support engagement, friction and remote stiffness can change the load path. Documenting those choices is essential to explaining why a simulated joint sees a particular force or moment.

Pressure, gravity and equilibrium

The assumptions work highlighted the need to represent pressure-end thrust at artificial cuts and account separately for contained-fluid weight. I am learning to use reaction and equilibrium checks to test the load setup. A model that solves successfully still needs those checks before its stress contours can be interpreted.

Global-to-local transfer and bolted contact

The local flange study depends on correctly transforming the global force and moment components at the chosen cut. The reference point, coordinate axes and signs must stay explicit. Bolt preload, its possible scatter and contact behaviour then influence separation and load redistribution. Torque-based tension estimates also carry uncertainty through the assumed nut factor.

Mesh convergence and sensitivity studies

The work has helped me distinguish mesh refinement from evidence that the quantity of interest has stabilised. The planned convergence study will track selected results through successive meshes. Thermal gradients, support cases, boundary stiffness and preload changes are separate sensitivities; comparing them helps identify whether a result is robust to uncertain inputs.

04

Soft skills & working habits

  • Separating evidence from assumptions. Maintaining detailed and critical assumption registers has helped me make the modelling basis reviewable. I am learning to identify the uncertain inputs that could change the conclusion and attach a verification action or a sensitivity study to each one.
  • Asking focused technical questions. The information-request work turns a broad need for more data into specific requests for geometry, operating history, support condition and bolting records. Connecting a request to its effect on the model makes the request easier to prioritise.
  • Explaining a conclusion with its supporting evidence. I am practising how to present geometry, load cases, equilibrium, convergence and sensitivity before discussing stress results. This gives a reviewer a clear route through the reasoning and shows which questions are still open.
05

Detailed updates

A record of documented work and learning through September 2026. Future verification and delivery steps are listed separately.

August–September 2026

Sensitivity and mesh-convergence workflow

The study plan prioritised the load setup and global mesh-convergence work before broader sensitivities. Further questions cover thermal gradients, support behaviour, boundary stiffness and joint preload, with attention to which output is being compared.

Learning: refinement and sensitivity answer different questions. Both need a defined quantity of interest and a consistent comparison basis.

27 August 2026

Making critical assumptions visible

The critical register identifies inputs most likely to affect reactions, flange separation, bolt loading or local stress. It connects each assumption to its current basis, its engineering significance and a required verification or sensitivity action.

Deliverable: a critical-assumptions register for review, alongside the more detailed technical record. High-impact uncertain inputs remain items to verify or bracket.

August 2026

Global geometry and local flange development

The modelling work developed the piping layout and a parametric local joint, including flange, fastener and ring/groove features. Parameter records and step-by-step instructions support a repeatable modelling workflow and make revisions easier to trace.

Learning: the transfer station and coordinate convention must be recorded with the geometry. Local stress interpretation depends on a verified global load transfer.
