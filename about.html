My background, values & direction

About me.

I’m Bongani Dindi. My background is in mechanical engineering at the University of Cape Town.

I’m driven by curiosity, a desire to become technically capable, and the opportunity to contribute to something larger than myself. I find purpose in work that challenges me to learn and creates practical value for other people.

What guides me

  • I learn deeply and broadly.
  • I take responsibility for my work.
  • I want my contribution to matter.

01

How I think and work

I’m naturally inquisitive. I enjoy understanding how a system works, why a decision was made, and how the details connect to the wider problem. I ask questions, seek out experienced people, and use unfamiliar work as an opportunity to expand my understanding.

I value both depth and breadth. I want a strong grasp of the fundamentals while remaining open to different industries, methods and perspectives. I’m willing to begin with practical tasks and learn from the ground up, because that experience strengthens my technical judgement.

I bring focus and persistence to a problem. I can become deeply absorbed in the details, and I’m learning to balance that concentration with priorities, deadlines and the needs of the wider team. I enjoy an active environment where there is meaningful work to do and room to take initiative.

02

What I’m working towards

Technical depth and sound judgement

I want to become someone who can take responsibility for complex engineering problems and make well-supported decisions. My goal is to keep strengthening the connection between theory, analysis and practical experience, while learning to recognise the limits of my knowledge and ask for the right evidence.

Responsibility, leadership and useful relationships

I want to grow into greater technical and project responsibility, earn the trust of the people I work with, and help others develop. I’m interested in the human and commercial side of engineering: understanding needs, communicating clearly, and building relationships that create value for everyone involved.

A contribution beyond my own progress

I see engineering as a way to contribute to the communities and systems around me. I want my work to support practical improvements in how people live and how technology is used. Energy systems particularly interest me, and I hope to combine technical capability with a clear understanding of the people a solution serves.

03

My technical skills

Mechanical design and practical development

I work with CAD tools including SolidWorks and Autodesk Fusion to develop parts, assemblies and parametric models. I bring experience with drawings, prototyping, 3D printing and workshop processes, and I’m interested in how design choices affect manufacture, assembly and use.

Analysis, modelling and verification

I use engineering principles and computational models to investigate mechanical behaviour. My experience includes stress analysis, finite element modelling, vibrations, fluid mechanics and thermal questions. I pay attention to assumptions, boundary conditions and verification because the usefulness of a result depends on how it was produced.

Programming, data and controls

I use Python, MATLAB, Excel and VBA for calculations, data analysis, automation and visualisation. I also have experience with Arduino, sensing and data logging. I enjoy building tools that make a technical problem easier to explore, and I’m developing my ability to write clear, reproducible code.

Requirements and technical communication

I work on turning an open-ended problem into requirements, a plan and a set of checks. I document decisions and assumptions, explain findings in reports, and use feedback to improve the work. I’m continuing to strengthen my ability to connect a technical result to the decision it needs to inform.

04

How I work with people

  • I listen before deciding. I’m comfortable speaking with different stakeholders, asking about their needs and adapting my explanation to their background. I value conversations that build understanding and lead to a useful outcome.
  • I approach disagreement respectfully. I try to understand the other person’s perspective and discuss concerns directly in a private, constructive setting. When more support is needed, I seek an appropriate person to help the team move forward.
  • I welcome experience and feedback. I recognise that useful knowledge comes from people with different technical and professional backgrounds. I’m willing to take guidance, reconsider my approach and learn from the people around me.
  • I take quality and safety seriously. I follow agreed procedures, raise concerns and seek expert help when I’m uncertain. I value supporting others while remaining accountable for the limits of my own competence.
05

What keeps me curious

My curiosity extends across the sciences and the arts. Alongside engineering, I enjoy drawing, painting, piano and writing, and I’m interested in literature, philosophy and psychology. I value the patience, observation and creativity that these interests encourage.

I want to keep developing as a whole person: technically capable, thoughtful in how I work with others, and willing to learn throughout my career.
